# watercolor-sketch

Hand-drawn sketchbook infographic with pencil outlines and soft watercolor fills

## Design Aesthetic

Hand-drawn study-note infographic style — pencil outlines with slightly imperfect lines, soft watercolor fills in pastel tones, clean white paper background with subtle texture. Layout is minimal and neat, sections connected by simple arrows. Cute doodle-style icons bring concepts to life. The overall feel is an educational notebook page: clean, modern, and approachable. Similar to sketchbook explainer diagrams or bullet journal illustrations.

## Background

- Color: Off-White (#FAFAF8)
- Texture: Subtle paper texture — feels like a quality sketchbook page, not digital

## Typography

### Primary Font (Headlines)

Bold hand-drawn lettering with confident pencil strokes. Slightly imperfect, organic letterforms — not typed, not mechanical. Dark charcoal color. Can include Chinese handwritten labels when content requires.

### Secondary Font (Body)

Smaller handwritten annotations in medium gray. Clean and legible, casual but neat — like tidy study notes. Occasional labels on arrows and callout lines. Legible at small sizes.

## Visual Elements

- Pencil-outline drawings with slightly imperfect, organic strokes
- Soft watercolor fills inside outlines (pastel, ~30-50% opacity)
- Cute doodle-style icons (database cylinders, gear icons, charts, targets, lightbulbs)
- Simple arrows connecting sections to show flow
- Small circled numbers or labels for step sequences
- Light shadows for depth on key elements
- Neat section boxes with rounded corners
- Occasional decorative doodles (stars, checkmarks, small banners)

## Style Rules

### Do

- Keep the white paper background dominant with generous whitespace
- Use pencil-style outlines on all illustrations — slightly wobbly, hand-drawn feel
- Fill shapes with soft watercolor washes, not solid colors
- Organize content into clear sections connected by arrows
- Pair doodle icons with concise text labels
- Maintain balanced composition — no section should overwhelm others
- Keep information hierarchy clear: title > sections > details

### Don't

- Use photorealism or 3D rendering
- Use dark backgrounds or heavy color fills
- Create perfectly geometric shapes or straight lines
- Overcrowd the slide — each section needs breathing room
- Add slide numbers, footers, or logos
- Use gradients, drop shadows, or digital effects
- Mix more than 3 watercolor accent colors per slide

## Best For

Educational explainers, concept overviews, process diagrams, study notes, onboarding visuals, tutorial walkthroughs, knowledge summaries, how-it-works infographics
